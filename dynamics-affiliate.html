What affiliate marketing actually is
From the Gaming Academy affiliate guide

Affiliate marketing is performance-based marketing: a third party — the affiliate — promotes an operator's product through its own channels and earns a commission on the result achieved. Payment is for real conversions — a registration, a deposit, actual play — never for impressions or clicks. In plain terms, the affiliate is the operator's "external salesforce": it directs customers to the casino or sportsbook and earns a reward every time a referred customer completes a target action.

The four commission models — when and how much you pay
From the Gaming Academy affiliate guide
CPA — Cost Per Acquisition
$50–500+ per player
A fixed one-time payout for every referred player who completes the target action — registration plus deposit. The operator bears the risk: if the player leaves quickly, the loss is the operator's. Predictable, immediate, simple to calculate — but the affiliate has no incentive to send quality players once the CPA is paid.
Ideal for: media buyers and paid traffic.
RevShare — Revenue Share
Casino 25–60% · Sports 15–40% of NGR
A percentage of the NGR generated by referred players, paid every month while the player stays active. Recurring income and a built-in incentive to send quality traffic — but volatile, dependent on player retention, and exposed to negative carryover.
Ideal for: SEO sites, content affiliates, long-term partners.
Hybrid — CPA + RevShare
Reduced CPA + typically 10–25% RevShare
A reduced one-time CPA plus a reduced monthly RevShare: the affiliate is rewarded for the acquisition and keeps earning from the players' revenue. The most flexible model and a win-win for both sides — but complex to calculate, and it requires a platform that supports multiple models at once.
Ideal for: top affiliates and experienced partners with quality traffic.
Fixed Fee
Fixed monthly payment
A fixed monthly sum for brand exposure — a placement, banner, review, or listing position. No linkage to results: guaranteed exposure, not guaranteed outcome. Predictable cost for the operator, stable income for the affiliate — with the payback risk sitting on the operator's side.
Ideal for: large media with an established audience.
The negative carryover nuance. Under RevShare, if a referred player wins big in a given month, the operator takes a loss and the affiliate's balance can go negative — the next month starts in the red. Many affiliates negotiate a "no negative carryover" condition — clarify it in every deal you sign.
Model matcher — pick the affiliate, get the deal
From the Gaming Academy affiliate guide
Recommended: CPA
Media buyers front their own ad spend and need immediate, predictable payouts per acquisition — a one-time CPA matches their cash cycle.

There is no single rule — the model follows the strategy, not the preference.

The operator side — what base do you calculate on?
From the Digitain bonus management guide
ModelCalculation baseTypical rateKey characteristic
Model 1GGR (gross revenue)25–35% of GGRSimplest calculation. Ignores bonuses and provider costs. Favourable to the affiliate — dangerous for the operator at high bonus budgets.
Model 2NGR (GGR − Bonuses − Payment)30–40% of NGRIndustry standard. Accounts for bonus load. Self-balancing: higher bonus = lower affiliate base.
Model 3ANGR (GGR − Bonuses − Game − Payment)25–40% of ANGRMost accurate base. Operator shares provider-cost risk with the affiliate. Rate is higher, but the real GGR burden is minimal.

The same deal on the same book — GGR €1,000,000, bonuses €250,000 (25%), game provider €120,000 (12%), payment €30,000 (3%). Pick a base:

€720,000 × 35% = €252,000 payout — 25.2% of GGR
STANDARD. The NGR base accounts for the bonus load, so the deal self-balances: the more you spend on bonuses, the lower the affiliate base.
The difference between Model 1 and Model 3 on €1M GGR is up to €90,000 per year — purely from choosing the right calculation base. At €10M GGR, that is €900,000 of additional NetPL.

The 8 affiliate fraud schemes — mechanism and key defenses
From the Digitain bonus management guide
1 · Self-Referral
Mechanism: the affiliate registers as a player via their own link — collecting the welcome bonus and a CPA for "acquiring" themselves. CPA €60 + €150 bonus = €210 lost per fake self-referral, with zero real acquisition; a 50-account ring means €10,500+.
Key defenses: cross-reference payment details of affiliate and referrals, KYC both parties, device fingerprinting between affiliate panel and player account, 30-day CPA payment delay.
2 · Fake Leads
Mechanism: purchased email/phone databases or bots drive mass registrations; accounts make a minimal deposit and vanish. CPA €40 × 500 fake FTDs = €20,000 paid out against €200–500 of real GGR.
Key defenses: delayed CPA — pay after 30–60 days only on real activity (minimum deposits, minimum GGR threshold); quality score per affiliate; auto-block when churn exceeds 80%.
3 · Cookie Stuffing
Mechanism: tracking cookies planted on users' devices without their knowledge — via iframes, pop-ups, ad networks — so the affiliate collects RevShare on organic players. One such affiliate can "claim" 10–20% of organic traffic.
Key defenses: server-to-server tracking instead of cookie-based, monitoring anomalous tracking/conversion ratios, last-click attribution with source verification, regular traffic audits.
4 · Incentivised Traffic
Mechanism: the affiliate pays users to register ("€10 for signing up"); they deposit the minimum with no genuine interest. LTV of such players: zero; churn after first deposit: 95%+.
Key defenses: prohibit incentivised traffic in the agreement with financial penalties, monitor 30-day behaviour (churn >90% from a source triggers review), set a minimum GGR-per-FTD quality threshold as a CPA condition.
5 · Brand Bidding
Mechanism: the affiliate buys search ads on your own brand keywords — users already looking for you click the ad and register through the affiliate link. Up to 30–40% of "new" FTDs in some regions are actually brand traffic — you pay RevShare for your own loyal users.
Key defenses: prohibit brand bidding in the agreement, monitor search ads, file trademark claims, audit UTM parameters, verify referral sources automatically at registration.
6 · RevShare Manipulation via Player Selection
Mechanism: the affiliate deliberately directs large winners to accumulate a negative balance, expecting it to be "reset" without carryover — then starts fresh next month and collects full RevShare again.
Key defenses: a strict negative carryover policy (negative balance always carried forward), cap monthly RevShare for new affiliates in the first 6 months, cap RevShare from any single player.
7 · Sub-Affiliate Fraud (Multi-Tier)
Mechanism: a master affiliate builds a sub-affiliate network in which some members run fraudulent schemes — the master earns an override commission while its own account looks clean; 10 fraudulent subs out of 50 can mean 20–30% fraudulent traffic.
Key defenses: contractual liability — the master answers for all sub-affiliate traffic; KYC for sub-affiliates above volume thresholds; the operator's right to audit the entire chain.
8 · Geo-Fraud
Mechanism: the affiliate agrees a high Tier-1 CPA (€80–150), then delivers Tier-3 traffic behind VPN/proxy to fake the GEO — the CPA difference is pure profit. 200 fake Tier-1 FTDs can mean £18,000+ in losses.
Key defenses: GEO verification via billing address, phone prefix, IP geolocation, and document origin at KYC; delay CPA until KYC completes; monitor anomalous GEO distribution per affiliate.
What every affiliate agreement must contain
From the Digitain bonus management guide
✓ Prohibition of all schemes
Every listed fraud scheme explicitly banned in the contract.
✓ Audit rights
The operator's right to audit traffic — including the entire sub-affiliate chain.
✓ Clawback clause
Commission recovery upon fraud detection, up to 12 months back.
✓ Negative carryover policy
Explicitly agreed — negative balances carried forward, not silently reset.
✓ Quality KPIs
Traffic-quality thresholds with the right to reduce RevShare when they are missed.
✓ Delayed CPA
Payment after 30–60 days, released only on real player activity.
